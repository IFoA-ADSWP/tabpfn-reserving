# Coverage over the fleet (#8) — the distribution is not calibrated

The fleet answered the question about the *centre* (`FINDINGS.md`: the point estimate is closer than Chain
Ladder on 38.8% of 464 evaluations). This measures the **spread**, which that result says nothing about — and
it is the claim this project actually rests on.

## What was measured

The same units, the same policy, the same column as the point-estimate run, so the two are directly
comparable: `IncurLoss`, the last three diagonals held out so the future being predicted is observed, the
direct arm's bar-bins draws (300 per unit), training labels clamped to the evaluation anchor.

```
containers scanned        775
  refused, not a triangle 136      (sparse or fragmented containers)
  usable triangles        639
coverage units            464      (horizons: 177 at 1 step, 148 at 2, 139 at 3)
skipped, all counted      1288 no scoreable future · 165 too few training rows · 136 not usable
model failures              0
```

Nothing was dropped silently and no failure is hidden.

## The result

| nominal | empirical | gap | 95% binomial CI | z |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50% | **39.2%** | −10.8pp | [34.8, 43.7] | −4.8 |
| 75% | **59.9%** | −15.1pp | [55.5, 64.4] | −6.6 |
| 90% | **78.0%** | −12.0pp | [74.2, 81.8] | −6.2 |
| 95% | **84.7%** | −10.3pp | [81.4, 88.0] | −6.2 |

**Under-coverage at every level, by 10–15 percentage points, at z between −4.8 and −6.6.** This is not noise
and not a single bad level: a confidently miscalibrated distribution.

By horizon it is similar throughout (90% nominal covering 78%, 85%, 71% at one, two and three steps), so it is
not a horizon artefact either.

## Why it misses, which the numbers also answer

**The intervals are not too narrow.** The median 90% interval is **4.55 times the point estimate** — enormous.
A wide interval that still misses the truth is centred in the wrong place, and that is exactly what the units
show:

> **the actual future exceeds the sampled median in 67.7% of units.**

Two-thirds of the time the truth is above the middle of the distribution. The under-coverage is therefore a
systematic **low centring**, and it is the same failure the point-estimate run measured from the other side
(the direct arm under-reserves on `genins` by 23.5% and `ukmotor` by 32.8%). One cause, two symptoms: intervals
that miss because the centre is low, and a point estimate that under-reserves.

**Sharpened since, at the tail where a risk margin is actually read: the failure is entirely one-sided.** Over
**all 464** units, **13.8%** have their truth above the model's own 95% upper bound against a nominal 2.5%, while
only **1.5%** fall below. *(The recalibration analysis in `CALIBRATION.md` reports the same quantity on the
231-unit held-out half it needed for fitting — 14.7% above and 0.9% below; the two estimates differ by well under
their own sampling error, se ≈1.6pp at n=464 and ≈2.3pp at n=231, and the full-sample pair is the canonical one.)*
So it is not that the intervals are wide and misplaced; the **upper tail is too short** and the lower tail is not.
That sharpening, three attempts to correct it, and the reason the one that appears to work is not a result:
`results/fleet/CALIBRATION.md` and **#21**. The figure `results/figures/tail_asymmetry.png` is this paragraph, and
every number on it is computed from the recorded units rather than typed in.

The figure (`results/figures/coverage_curve.png`) is the same statement geometrically: every point below the
diagonal, most error bars clear of it, regenerated by `python scripts/make_figures.py`.

## What this settles, and — more importantly — what it does not

**Settled.** The model's predictive distribution is not calibrated on real triangles. The entry's headline
claim cannot be "a reserve *with its distribution*" without that sentence attached to it.

**Not settled, and this is the honest limit of the claim: there is no comparison against the classical
intervals.** Mack's method and the ODP bootstrap produce intervals too, and nothing here measures whether
*they* cover 90% on these same units. A raw coverage gap of −12pp is a serious finding about this model; it is
**not** evidence that the model is worse than the standard method, because the standard method has not been
measured on the same scale. That comparison is the obvious next experiment and is filed as **#20**.

**And one thing the mechanism does establish.** The draws are taken from the model's own bar distribution by
inverse-CDF sampling, and the arithmetic underneath reproduces the CAS package to the pound. So this is *the
model's* miscalibration, not an artefact of how the distribution was sampled — a distinction that had to be
earned earlier, when an interpolated 15-level grid was clamping the tail and understating the `abc` p99 by 62%.
